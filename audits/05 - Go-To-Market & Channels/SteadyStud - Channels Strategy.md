# SteadyStud — Channels strategy (draft v0.2)

## Category reality check
Magnetic stud finders are a known commodity. To win, SteadyStud must be **visibly different** (marking notch + ease) and marketed with simple proof.

## Competitive pricing signal (initial)
A major existing magnetic stud finder (StudBuddy) is listed at **$10.97** at Home Depot (per public listing snippet).

Source: Brave search result snippet pointing to Home Depot listing: https://www.homedepot.com/p/The-StudBuddy-Magnetic-Stud-Finder-000107/205554450

(We will not treat the snippet as ground truth; we’ll confirm via additional sources like Amazon price bands + multiple retailers.)

## Best channel candidates (ranked for an early physical product)
### 1) Amazon (launch + validation)
**Why:** high purchase intent; easy to test demand; reviews + video can sell the difference.
**Risks:** commoditized competition; requires strong differentiation, tight listing, and review strategy.

### 2) DTC (brand-building)
**Why:** better brand story, higher margin potential, bundles (e.g., small mounting kit), collect emails.
**Risks:** you must generate demand; CAC can overwhelm a low-ASP tool unless you go viral/UGC.

### 3) Hardware retail (Home Depot/Lowe’s/ACE)
**Why:** perfect context; impulse purchase.
**Risks:** hard to enter; packaging/UPC/planograms; you need velocity proof.

## What to test first (low-cost, high-signal)
1. **Messaging test**: “Find studs instantly. No batteries. Mark center with notch.”
2. **Price band test** (hypothesis): $12–$19. If your experience is premium and notch is unique, you can likely sit above the $10–$12 commodity baseline.
3. **Video demo**: 10–20 seconds: slide → snap → mark.

## Recommendation (tentative)
- **Phase 1:** Amazon + simple DTC landing page (email capture) in parallel.
- **Phase 2:** Use Amazon traction + reviews to approach retail.
