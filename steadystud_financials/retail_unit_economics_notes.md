# SteadyStud — Retail unit economics notes (draft)

## Why retail is the endgame
Retail can deliver scale and defensibility (distribution relationships + shelf presence), but it imposes hard constraints on unit economics and packaging.

## Key rule of thumb to validate
Wholesale pricing often lands near ~50% of MSRP for many consumer products (varies by category, distributor, and retailer). If a distributor is used, their margin further compresses manufacturer margin.

## Current placeholder economics (from assumptions.csv)
- MSRP (DTC target): $18.99

- COGS ex-finish: $5.00
- Finish: $1.50

Wholesale gross margin per unit (before freight, chargebacks, returns):
$9.50 - $5.00 - $1.50 - $0.70 = $2.30

## Implication
At current placeholders, retail margin is thin. To make retail a long-term profit engine, we likely need at least one of:
- lower COGS (quote-driven)
- modestly higher MSRP (if packaging + differentiation support it)
- bundle/kit that raises MSRP and increases perceived value
- reducing finishing cost or making finishing optional in the retail SKU
